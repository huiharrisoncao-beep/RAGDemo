1What is RAG

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) = Retrieval + Generation.

An LLM's knowledge is bounded by its training data and prone to hallucination. RAG's idea: before answering, retrieve relevant material from an external corpus, put it into the prompt, then let the LLM generate an answer grounded in that material.

🔑 The crux: can the retriever fetch all the material actually needed to answer? This is exactly where traditional RAG and GraphRAG diverge.

The basic retrieve → generate loop

2How Traditional RAG Works

Retrieval by vector similarity: turn text into vectors, compare who is closest.

Five Steps

  • ① Chunk split corpus into text chunks
  • ② Embed encode each chunk into a vector
  • ③ Index store vectors (Faiss)
  • ④ Retrieve embed the question, find top-k nearest
  • ⑤ Generate stitch context, hand to the LLM

Key trait: flat similarity matching —— chunks have no relational structure between them.

3The Structural Limitation of Traditional RAG

A multi-hop question

"My favorite movie — where is its director from?"

To answer, first find "who the director is", then "where the director was born" —— two facts often live in different documents:

Four structural problems

  • Multi-hop breaks: hops 2/3 usually lack the question's keywords → missed
  • No relational structure: cannot "follow a relation onward"
  • No aggregation / traversal: no mechanism for "a class of neighbors + filter"
  • Weak explainability, prone to hallucination: models tend to "fill in" missing context

⚠️ This is not a tuning issue. Increasing top-k only adds noise; later hops stay dissimilar —— a structural flaw of flat similarity retrieval.

4How GraphRAG Works

Replace the "flat set of chunks" with an entity-relation graph: retrieval = walking paths along relations on the graph.

Four Steps

  • ① Extract LLM extracts triples (subject, relation, object)
  • ② Build connect triples into a directed graph (with source)
  • ③ Traverse walk multiple hops from the question entity, collect the whole chain
  • ④ Generate hand the path as context to the LLM

Key difference: later hops are found precisely via edges, even when literally dissimilar.

5GraphRAG Advantages & the Problems It Solves

AdvantageWhat it solvesWhy traditional RAG can't
Multi-hop reasoning"C of B of A" — chaining several factslater hops dissimilar to the query, missed
Cross-document linkingclues scattered across documents, need stitchingsimilarity scores chunks independently, ignores links
Aggregation queries"which subsidiary CEO meets a condition" — traverse + filterno "traverse a class of neighbors", only guesses top-k
Explainabilityneed to show "how the answer was derived"only returns chunks, can't state the reasoning chain
Anti-hallucinationreduce fabricationwith partial context the model tends to "fill in" → hallucinate

Multi-hop reasoning and aggregation queries are qualitative leaps —— not "a bit better", but "one can, the other simply cannot".

6Corpus Design: 5 Docs Sliced into 19 Chunks

Split by blank lines, skip pure heading blocks, one paragraph = one chunk (see common/corpus.py). Each doc's #1/#6/#8/#12/#16 is an isolation statement, and the body deliberately omits the next hop's keywords.

companies.md (5 chunks) #1 This doc only describes Xinghuan's equity & subsidiary structure; no exec/personnel info. #2 Xinghuan is a tech conglomerate spanning AI, data platforms & networking, managing subsidiaries via holdings. #3 Yunshu AI is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Xinghuan, focused on enterprise AI inference. #4 Hengxing Data is a subsidiary of Xinghuan, doing large-scale data warehousing & real-time analytics. #5 Guangnian Net also belongs to Xinghuan, covering networking & edge computing. investors.md (2 chunks) #6 This doc only records investment relations between investors and invested companies. #7 Panshi Capital is a tech-focused investor that invested in Xinghuan, becoming a key shareholder.
leadership.md (4 chunks) #8 This doc only records CEO appointments; no education background or alma mater. #9 Li Ming is the CEO of Yunshu AI, in charge of product & operations. #10 Wang Fang is the CEO of Hengxing Data, leading its data-platform strategy. #11 Chen Wei is the CEO of Guangnian Net, running its networking business. people.md (4 chunks) #12 This doc only records person→alma mater; not the school's city, nor their employer. #13 Li Ming graduated from Weiming Tech, majoring in CS. ← no "Yunshu AI" #14 Wang Fang graduated from Mingde Univ. with an info-eng degree. #15 Chen Wei graduated from Beishan Univ., specializing in comms eng. universities.md (4 chunks) #16 This doc only records university→city; no people or companies. #17 Weiming Tech sits in Jiangcheng, strong in engineering. ← no "Yunshu"/"Li Ming" #18 Mingde Univ. is in Songdao, famed for information sciences. #19 Beishan Univ. is in Yungang, strong in comms & electronics.

Chaining #9→#13→#17: #13 has no "Yunshu AI", #17 has neither keyword → traditional RAG only fetches #9; the other two are necessarily missed —— a reproducible demonstration of the structural limit.

6Why It Fails: Chain-Break Analysis

Q: Yunshu AI's CEO graduated from where? Which city?

Three root causes

  • Only hop 1 is similar to the query: the question has "Yunshu AI / CEO", only #9 hits
  • Hops 2/3 are inherently dissimilar: #13/#17 lack the question's keywords → far in vector space → missed
  • Raising top-k doesn't help: it only adds noise (e.g. #19); later hops stay dissimilar

⚠️ Partial context → the LLM either can't answer, or "fills in" the gap → hallucination.

This is a structural flaw of flat similarity retrieval —— not fixable by tuning.

7GraphRAG Step-by-Step · Step 1: Extract

The LLM turns each document into triples (subject — relation → object), recording the source document.

Xinghuan --HAS_SUBSIDIARY--> Yunshu AI [companies.md] Xinghuan --HAS_SUBSIDIARY--> Hengxing Data [companies.md] Xinghuan --HAS_SUBSIDIARY--> Guangnian Net [companies.md] Panshi Capital --INVESTED_IN--> Xinghuan [investors.md] Yunshu AI --HAS_CEO--> Li Ming [leadership.md] Hengxing Data --HAS_CEO--> Wang Fang [leadership.md] Guangnian Net --HAS_CEO--> Chen Wei [leadership.md] Li Ming --GRADUATED_FROM--> Weiming Tech [people.md] Wang Fang --GRADUATED_FROM--> Mingde Univ. [people.md] Chen Wei --GRADUATED_FROM--> Beishan Univ. [people.md] Weiming Tech --LOCATED_IN--> Jiangcheng [universities.md] Mingde Univ. --LOCATED_IN--> Songdao [universities.md] Beishan Univ. --LOCATED_IN--> Yungang [universities.md] —— 13 triples total

Fixed relation set (teaching build)

  • HAS_SUBSIDIARY parent → subsidiary
  • HAS_CEO company → CEO
  • GRADUATED_FROM person → university
  • LOCATED_IN university → city
  • INVESTED_IN investor → company

All three intermediate steps are printed, so you can watch live how it walks the chain.

7GraphRAG Step-by-Step · Step 3: Multi-Hop Traversal (Q1)

Start from "Yunshu AI", walk along edges

[GraphRAG] ③ multi-hop from start entity ['Yunshu AI']: hop 1: Yunshu AI --HAS_CEO--> Li Ming hop 2: Li Ming --GRADUATED_FROM--> Weiming Tech hop 3: Weiming Tech --LOCATED_IN--> Jiangcheng [generation] answer: Yunshu AI's CEO is Li Ming, who graduated from Weiming Tech, which is located in Jiangcheng.

The whole chain walks precisely along edges —— no reliance on similarity

7Contrast · Q2 Aggregation Traversal (expand to a tree + filter)

Q2: Among all Xinghuan subsidiary CEOs, whose alma mater is in Jiangcheng?

hop 1: Xinghuan --HAS_SUBSIDIARY--> Yunshu AI / Hengxing / Guangnian hop 2: Yunshu→Li Ming Hengxing→Wang Fang Guangnian→Chen Wei hop 3: Li Ming→Weiming Wang Fang→Mingde Chen Wei→Beishan hop 4: Weiming→Jiangcheng Mingde→Songdao Beishan→Yungang ⇒ filter "city = Jiangcheng" ⇒ Li Ming ✓

The essence: hops 2/3 that traditional RAG misses are explicit edges in GraphRAG. Traversal follows edges, not "how similar" —— so the chain never breaks. That is the root reason it achieves multi-hop.

Traverse + filter by city ⇒ Li Ming

8Boundaries & Trade-offs

GraphRAG is not a silver bullet; it has costs too.

DimensionTraditional RAGGraphRAG
Build costlow (embedding only)high (needs LLM triple extraction)
Strong atsingle-hop, fact lookup, semantic searchmulti-hop, aggregation, relational reasoning
Depends onembedding qualityextraction quality + relation design
Explainabilityweakstrong (explicit paths)

💡 In practice they are often combined —— vector retrieval for coarse recall, graph structure for relational reasoning. This Demo separates them to sharpen the contrast.

⬡Demo Architecture Overview

One shared common/ layer + two parallel pipelines: RAG does vector retrieval, GraphRAG does graph traversal, and both hand off to the same ChatClient to generate the answer.

▶Run the Demo

Three commands

# Traditional RAG python RAG/rag_qa.py # GraphRAG (extracts & caches on first run) python GraphRAG/graphrag_qa.py # Side-by-side comparison python compare.py

Config essentials

  • chat uses an OpenAI-compatible API (e.g. DeepSeek), reads .env
  • embedding is decoupled from chat: local model by default, works offline
  • corpus CORPUS_LANG=cn|en switches in one flag, capability is language-agnostic
  • extraction result is cached as graph.json, human-reviewable

See README.md and SHARING.md for details.
